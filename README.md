# Objective

OpenReproLab teaches first-year PhD students the industry-grade computing tools and methods to produce FAIR and reproducible science. More generally, this programme fosters a culture of scientific openness and transparency, where all relevant data and software produced during the scientific activity are freely accessible and reusable, well-documented, and citable, and where all published results are fully reproducible.

# Content

We acknowledge that computing skills are best learned when working on real projects. OpenReproLab will be structured around lab sessions ("travaux pratiques") where participants will solve practical problems related to producing FAIR and reproducible science.

## Lab sessions

During lab sessions, which are similar in design to data/coding challenges, each participant will work on practical problems in the presence of one or more instructors to answer their questions and guide their learning. Lab sessions may involve, for example, writing computer programs, configuring computing environments, and writing documentation or metadata. Students will submit their work via git commits on a git-hosting online platform. Real-time automated feedback will be given to students via the platform, so that they can validate their work, learn from their mistakes, and keep trying until they get it right. All necessary material will be provided for each lab session but, to the extent it is possible, participants will be given the option to use their own PhD research material instead (eg. their own data sets).

**Volume:** Seven 2-hour lab sessions (summary lecture + practice), cf. programme below

## Summary lectures

Each lab session will start with a summary lecture on the day’s relevant topics.

**Volume:** The first 30-45 minutes of each lab session.

## Autonomous work

Prior to each lab session, participants will be asked to acquire knowledge autonomously on the topic at hand via readings and/or online tutorial videos. For each topic, participants will be given a one-page summary of the key points and take-home messages, along with the list of readings/videos to prepare for the corresponding lab session.

**Volume:** About two hours of autonomous work prior to each lab session.

## Study sessions

In order to encourage and facilitate the autonomous work required of participants prior to each lab, we will organize optional study sessions where students can gather up to do the work together. These sessions will be unsupervised (no instructor present) so as to make it a safe space for students to interact among peers.

**Volume:** Two hours of study session, one week before each lab session.

## Office hours

We will hold regular office hours, where students can come without appointment to talk one-on-one with instructors about any topic relevant to OpenReproLab, including asking for clarification on past material, asking for help on unfinished lab sessions, discussing more advanced topics, and how to apply OpenReproLab principles to their own research.

**Volume:** Two hours (one instructor) per month.

## Information meetings

A few plenary meetings will be necessary for organizational purposes, such as a first "welcome" meeting, to greet everyone and present the programme.

**Volume:** Three 1-hour meeting (welcome meeting + intermediate meetings + final meeting).

## Social events

Social events with optional attendance will be organised to foster cohort cohesion and facilitate long-lasting professional connections among participants. These will be held on campus, in the mid- to late-afternoon. IGE will provide snacks and soft drinks.

**Volume:** Two social events

# List of lab sessions

 1. First steps in numerical science: Git basics, writing a Python script, writing a Jupyter Notebook

 2. Data management: metadata and documentation, traceability, CF conventions, DOI, versioning, data warehouses.

 3. Code management, part 1: licensing, modularity, documentation.

 4. Code management, part 2: managing dependencies and computing environments.

 5. Team-oriented development: leveraging Git and online Git-hosting platforms to develop software as a team, git branching, pull requests and code reviews, documentation, governance.

 6. Reproducibility challenge: try to reproduce results from a real-life scientific paper, analyse what limits reproducibility, propose solutions.

 7. Open science and artificial intelligence: using large language models to produce scientific code: use cases, recommendations, risks and pitfalls, accountability.

# Capacity and eligibility

OpenReproLab will accept up to 25 participants. Are eligible, from highest to lowest priority:

 - IGE PhD students who started in calendar year 2026

 - IGE PhD students who started in calendar year 2025

 - IGE IE/IR BAP E employed as CDD

# Hours of "formation transversale / disciplinaire" for PhD students

For PhD students, this programme is worth 13 hours among the 120 hours of mandatory training. We will deliver an attestation of training completion to students who have attended 6 out of the 7 lab sessions in person and who have completed all the work for the 7 lab sessions. This work can be finished after the lab sessions have ended, but no later than May 20th.

# Accommodations for disability

We intend to make OpenReproLab accessible to everyone. If you have a disability that makes this programme less accessible to you, please let us know as soon as possible, so we can implement relevant accommodations. We respect your privacy and we will not share any disability-related information outside of the OpenReproLab organisers.
